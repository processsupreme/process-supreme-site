The idea

Nobody has the person in the room.

In cannabis retail the ad does not move product. The budtender does, standing next to the customer at the moment they decide. Brands that win certify, equip and pay that person. I spent fifteen years on the retail side of that, in a category that could not buy Google, Meta or TV. The effect is measurable: when we ran the Budtender Incentive Program across our Nevada stores, the brands in it saw sales rise 72% during the incentive window while brands that sat out fell 12.5%.

Prediction markets have the same shape. Everyone is buying affiliate codes, TV and stadium signage. The sports version of the budtender is the bartender at a sports bar in Texas on a Saturday. Texas and Georgia have never had a legal book and Florida has one monopoly app, so nobody has ever marketed at that level, and the person pouring the beer is about to get asked "who you got?"

The coaster sells the whole app: Drafts, Pick'em, Prediction Picks, Combos, Rips, all live in all three states. It leads with "who you got?" because Pick'em is a group chat question, and Team Picks, spreads and totals are the first Underdog product that answers a bar question. Whoever scans gets everything. The program measures the first deposit.

How it works

Five steps. Same as a budtender program.

  1. 1

    Recruit

    Ten home bars per metro, fifty to start, from the list below. Signed in person by a rep. Manager first, staff opt in.

  2. 2

    Certify

    Twenty minutes and a staff card: what a contract is, who can play, what not to say. No certification, no code.

  3. 3

    Equip

    Coasters, a table tent, the staff card, a printed chalkboard strip for the week's big game. Nothing that needs Wi-Fi, a tablet or a login.

  4. 4

    Pay

    Staff earn per verified first deposit, paid weekly, with a second payment at day thirty. Bars get a funded game night every month and compete for Home Bar of the Month. The customer gets only Underdog's standard offer.

  5. 5

    Measure

    Every bar is its own P&L against the affiliate cohort. Weekly readout. A bar that is not producing by week six gets swapped for the next one on the list.

Incentives

Pay the bartender like a budtender.

Fifteen dollars a head reads like a survey payout, and five hundred dollars split across a thirty person staff is lunch. The fix is not more cash. It is immediacy for the individual and traffic for the bar, which is exactly how the good cannabis brands run their budtender programs.

Every bartender

$25 on every verified first deposit, paid weekly. $25 more when that customer is still active at day thirty. $100 bonus at ten verified in a month.

A bartender doing six a week clears about $1,100 a month in season for handing out coasters and answering one question.

Top bartender

Each metro, each month: $500 and two tickets to the metro's game. Cowboys, Texans, Longhorns, Falcons, Bucs. Names on the metro leaderboard every week, because bartenders are competitive and everybody can see it.

Season end: the top bartender across all five metros wins a trip to the Super Bowl in Los Angeles in February. Two tickets, flights and the hotel.

The bar

A $500 game night every month, spent on the crowd (first round, wings) on the biggest game on the calendar. That is money the owner sees in the register.

Home Bar of the Month per metro: Underdog pushes the bar to its users in that city and picks up a staff party tab after close. Season end, the top bar across all metros hosts a Super Bowl party on Underdog's tab. Bars want traffic and a night out for the staff, not a check.

Rewards to staff are cash and experiences, never credit in the app, so nobody in the program is paid in the product. Staff never handle money or place an entry for anyone. They hand out a coaster and answer a question.

Compliance

Built for a regulated product.

Underdog runs under more than one rulebook: state fantasy law for Drafts and Pick'em, federal rules for Prediction Picks, and age and location rules on all of it. The program is designed so it never has to care which product the customer opens, and so nothing a bartender does could be read as running the product.

  • The customer gets only the standard offerNo Regulars bonus, no bar-only boost, no prize for playing, no leaderboard of customers.
  • Staff are marketing partners, not agentsPaid outside the app in cash and experiences, never in credit. Same attribution model as the promo codes running on CBS and Saturday Down South today, pointed at a bartender instead of a URL. Staff never handle money, place an entry, or trade for anyone.
  • Leaderboards rank bars and bartendersOn verified signups and day-thirty actives. Never on what anyone plays.
  • Fixed copy, legal firstStaff read from the card: 18 and up, in state only, and a never-say list. Legal signs off on the structure, the coaster and the card before the first bar is signed. Every version, certification and prize lives in the compliance file.

Base lands at the bottom of that band fully loaded, high is under it, and low is the kill signal: if bars are doing two a week, the fixed field cost eats it and the program stops at fifty. The 2027 arithmetic, not a promise: 2,000 bars at the base rate is about 29,000 first deposits a month in season, roughly 350,000 a year, against about 950,000 monthly actives today.

The system

Seven engines under fifty bars.

A coaster is a piece of cardboard. What makes it a channel is the machinery that turns a scan into a verified first deposit, a weekly payout, a leaderboard, and a bar-level P&L you can put next to the affiliate cohort. I build that machinery the same way I build it for multi-unit retailers: every location is its own business, every dollar is attributed, and the readout fits on one page.

Underdog plugs in three things: a referral code per bartender from the affiliate plumbing, the verified first deposit and day-30 activity events by code, and ARPU per retained depositor for the contribution line. The system never touches trading data.

Registry

Every bar, every certified bartender, their code, certification date, kit status and game night calendar. Nothing goes live without a certification record. The swap list lives here.

Attribution

One link and code per bartender, generated from the same plumbing the promo-code affiliates use. Verified first deposits arrive on the same feed. Rules: no self-referrals, one device one code, clawback on chargebacks, and a fraud flag when a code's day-30 rate falls off a cliff.

Payout ledger

Weekly. $25 on a verified first deposit, $25 at day thirty, the threshold bonus, the top-bartender prizes. 1099s handled, every line auditable for legal.

Leaderboards

Bar and bartender by metro, refreshed weekly, visible to staff and reps, never to customers, never touching trading activity.

Field ops

The rep app: visit logs, restocks, certification sessions, game night scheduling, a bar health score. One rep covers sixty bars because the app tells them which ten need a visit this week.

Bar P&L

Every bar, every month: first deposits, day-30 actives, staff pay, kit, game night, share of rep cost. Cost per deposit and per retained depositor against the affiliate cohort, contribution using your ARPU. The same store-level P&L a seven-store retailer runs.

Compliance file

Every version of the coaster and card, every certification, every prize, mapped to each rulebook it touches. When legal asks, it is one export, not a scramble.
